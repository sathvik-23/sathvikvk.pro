Why Robotics Demos Lie

Not literally dishonest. A demo answers a different question from deployment.

Investment question

Can a robotics company move from an impressive task demonstration to reliable, paid, repeatable industrial deployment?

Core argument

Model accuracy is only one part of the investment case. Deployment time, uptime, integration burden, service intensity, working capital and customer payback determine whether the company can scale.

DEMO → DEPLOYMENT
Demotask works once
Pilotuptime, integration, operator workflow
ProductionPO, repeat cells, payback

Flagship implication

CynLr is PROCEED SUBJECT TO CONDITIONS because the commercial conversion milestone matters more than the demo. The model prices the exposure as an option: one-third at close, two-thirds after the production milestone.

Counterargument

Milestone financing can miss the upside if the company’s next round reprices quickly. It also does not remove the risk that a successful pilot remains a one-off deployment.

What would prove the view wrong?

Multiple production POs, measured customer payback and improving deployment time would prove that the company has crossed from technical promise into repeatable industrial product.
