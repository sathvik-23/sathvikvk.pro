What would make me back a pre-revenue AI founder?

What evidence can exist before revenue?

Investment question

At pre-revenue, I back the founder’s insight only when it creates a fast, falsifiable path to customer evidence.

Core argument

The founder must see a painful constraint, name the first customer and design a test that can disprove the idea quickly. Compute economics, power, data movement, specialised silicon and physical deployment become more important precisely because intelligence is becoming more abundant.

Evidence

  • At this stage, customer interviews and product tests are more probative than revenue proxies.
  • The research universe treats unavailable founder and customer evidence as Unknown.

Counterargument

Incumbent platforms may reach down and commoditise many apparent bottlenecks. A physical interface is not automatically a moat; the company must own deployment data, customer integration or a hard-to-replace component.

What would prove this wrong?

If compute-layer companies consistently capture the margin while physical-interface companies remain sub-scale component vendors, the thesis is wrong. I would update on gross-margin trends, design-in conversion and strategic acquisition evidence.

Investment implication

Track the bottleneck, then underwrite the ownership and price. A good company is not necessarily a good venture investment.
