When does a successful pilot become commercial evidence?

A pilot is a hypothesis, not a business.

Investment question

The investment question is whether the pilot changes the customer’s operating behaviour and creates a repeatable buying path.

Core argument

Commercial evidence begins when a buyer pays, repeats and can explain the ROI without the founder in the room. Compute economics, power, data movement, specialised silicon and physical deployment become more important precisely because intelligence is becoming more abundant.

PILOT TO REVENUE
Pilottechnical success
Commercial evidencepaid repeat use
Repeatabilitybuyer pull × ROI × sales cycle

Evidence

  • For physical AI, the relevant trigger is a pilot converting to a production purchase order with measured uptime and payback.
  • A single logo or sponsored demonstration is not enough to establish repeatability.

Counterargument

Incumbent platforms may reach down and commoditise many apparent bottlenecks. A physical interface is not automatically a moat; the company must own deployment data, customer integration or a hard-to-replace component.

What would prove this wrong?

If compute-layer companies consistently capture the margin while physical-interface companies remain sub-scale component vendors, the thesis is wrong. I would update on gross-margin trends, design-in conversion and strategic acquisition evidence.

Investment implication

Track the bottleneck, then underwrite the ownership and price. I would update toward investable demand when deployments repeat across customers with less bespoke effort.
